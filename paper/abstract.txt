The Gamma function is a function that appears throughout statistics, particularly in
Bayesian inference over Gamma, Beta, and Dirichlet distributions. Modern Bayesian algorithms
such as variational inference repeatedly evaluate the logarithm of the Gamma
function and its derivatives, making both approximation accuracy and derivative quality
important considerations for computational efficiency. This paper introduces a framework
for constructing identity-constrained approximations of special functions. In contrast to
fitting an approximation through pointwise error minimization, the proposed framework
incorporates functional identities directly into the optimization objective. These identities
serve as structural constraints that enforce global properties of the target function beyond
local accuracy. The framework is demonstrated using the logarithm of the Gamma function
as a case study through Bohr-Mollerup characterization, Euler recurrence relation, and Legendre
duplication identity. Three optimization objectives are investigated: least-squares
fitting, duplication-identity fitting, and a composite identity-constrained objective that
balances approximation accuracy with structural identity preservation. A second phase
evaluates these approximations in Bayesian inference using a Dirichlet-Multinomial variational
inference benchmark. Because the Dirichlet posterior parameters can be computed
exactly, every approximation is evaluated at the exact posterior parameters, isolating approximation
error from optimization error. Performance is measured using Evidence Lower
Bound (ELBO) accuracy together with gradient accuracy, evaluated through direct access
to Log-Gamma, Digamma, and Trigamma functions. Experimental results demonstrate
that identity-constrained approximations consistently provide the strongest performance
within variational inference. In contrast, least-squares approximations generally achieve
superior accuracy over a bounded grid of points. These findings suggest that preserving
functional identities can improve downstream optimization performance even when
traditional approximation error is not minimized among available models. The proposed
framework extends to transformed Gamma functions, multivariate Gamma-related functions,
and other special functions characterized by functional identities, providing a general
methodology for constructing structurally informed approximation for use in computational
statistics.
